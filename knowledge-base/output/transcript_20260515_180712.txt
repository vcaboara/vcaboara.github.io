# Critical Review: WIPO GREEN 'Patent/Know-how information' Field

## Initial Risk Assessment

Before drafting, I need to flag several concerns that require resolution:

### RISK A: Pre-Disclosure Risk — HIGH CONCERN

**The fundamental tension:** WIPO GREEN is a public database designed to facilitate technology transfer. Any text entered becomes globally visible and indexed. This creates a direct conflict with continuation patent strategy.

**Specific risks I'm flagging:**

1. **Process sequencing detail**: The knowledge base states the innovation is "novel configuration of existing off-the-shelf industrial hardware." If you describe the *specific sequence* or *configuration logic* in this public field, you may be creating prior art against your own continuation applications.

2. **Self-powered mechanism**: The self-powered steady-state operation appears to be a key differentiator. Describing *how* this is achieved (e.g., using pyrolysis gases to fuel subsequent processing) could foreclose patent claims on the energy integration method.

3. **The filed patent is 5 months old**: Has the US application published yet? If not (typically 18 months from filing), you're potentially disclosing unpublished claims. If it *has* published, then the claims themselves are already public and can be referenced.

**Question requiring your answer**: Has US 19/424,106 been published? If no, we should reference only the application number and filing date, NOT claim substance.

---

### RISK B: Know-How Scope — MODERATE CONCERN

Know-how that accompanies the patent license but isn't in the patent claims is valuable *precisely because* it's not public. Listing it in detail here commoditizes it.

**What's safe to mention:**
- That configuration parameters exist
- That integration protocols exist
- That compliance documentation exists

**What's NOT safe to detail:**
- Specific parameter ranges
- Specific integration sequences
- Specific compliance pathways (these have independent commercial value)

---

### RISK C: Trade Secret Boundary — HIGH CONCERN

**My strong recommendation: DO NOT mention Phase 2 at all.**

Reasons:
1. Even stating "additional know-how available under NDA" signals that something valuable exists beyond the filed patent
2. This invites reverse-engineering attempts
3. It creates a discoverable record that Phase 2 existed as of this date (relevant if Phase 2 is ever litigated)
4. WIPO GREEN is not the venue for trade secret positioning — it's for patent/tech transfer

**The exception:** If you want to signal that the licensable package is larger than what's publicly visible, you can say "additional technology portfolio available to qualified licensees" without specifying *what* that portfolio contains.

---

### RISK D: Attorney Optics — CRITICAL

A licensing attorney reviewing this field will look for:

1. **Coherence**: Does the description match the patent title and technical field?
2. **Completeness**: Is this a licensable package or a half-baked concept?
3. **Professionalism**: Is the language precise or vague/promotional?
4. **Red flags**: Overclaims, inconsistencies, signs of inexperience

**What builds confidence:**
- Direct reference to the patent application number and filing date
- Clear statement of what the licensee receives
- Acknowledgment of current development stage (TRL 3)
- Professional, understated tone

**What triggers skepticism:**
- Promotional language ("revolutionary," "groundbreaking")
- Vague claims about market impact
- Overstating readiness (implying pilot-ready when it's TRL 3)
- Mentioning trade secrets in a public database (looks amateur)

---

## My Recommendation: Intentionally Sparse Approach

Given the above risks, I recommend a **protective minimalist draft** that:

1. Establishes credibility (patent number, filing date, entity)
2. States technical scope at the level already public (if published) or abstract-level (if unpublished)
3. Describes licensable deliverables categorically, not substantively
4. Acknowledges development stage honestly
5. Does NOT describe process sequencing, energy integration mechanics, or trade secrets
